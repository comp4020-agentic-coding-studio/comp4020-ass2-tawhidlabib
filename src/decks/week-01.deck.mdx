# The eight rules

SLOP3908 · Week 1

---

## Three settings, not two

- **First place** — home
- **Second place** — work
- **Third place** — the other one

Oldenburg's claim: a life needs all three, and most people now have two.

---

## The eight

1. Neutral ground
2. A leveller
3. Conversation is the main activity
4. Accessible and accommodating
5. The regulars
6. A low profile
7. The mood is playful
8. A home away from home

---

## 1. Neutral ground

Nobody hosts. Nobody is obliged to be there and nobody can be made to stay.

You can leave without explaining, and arrive without an invitation.

*Which of the places you can think of fail this?*

---

## 2. A leveller

Rank at the door does not travel into the room.

The consultant and the delivery driver are both just people at the bar.

*Ask this again in week 4. It is the claim that survives least well.*

---

## 3. Conversation is the main activity

Not eating, not watching, not working alongside strangers.

Talk, as the point rather than the accompaniment.

*Week 7 breaks this one.*

---

## 4. Accessible and accommodating

Open when you need it. Near enough to reach without planning.

Nothing to book, nothing to bring.

*Week 9: what does "accessible" mean when the door is a graphics card?*

---

## 5. The regulars

The place is set by who is usually in it — not by the owner, not by the decor.

A newcomer is absorbed by the regulars or not at all.

*Thursday you will try to count them, and discover you cannot define them.*

---

## 6. A low profile

Plain. Unselfconscious. Not designed to be a third place.

The ones built to be community hubs mostly are not.

*This is the course's closing argument, twelve weeks early.*

---

## 7. The mood is playful

Wit over seriousness. Nobody is closing.

Anxiety is the thing the room is supposed to remove.

---

## 8. A home away from home

Rootedness, warmth, possession without ownership.

*Your* seat. Not because anyone said so.

# What is missing?

---

## Nothing here says who is kept out

Eight characteristics. Not one of them about the boundary.

Hold that thought until week 3 — and then week 4 will tell you the warmth and
the edge are the same mechanism.

---

## Thursday

Take a place you already go to.

- forty unbroken minutes
- write **in the room**
- describe before you explain
- come back knowing which of the eight it fails

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Read Oldenburg before you go

You are not scoring the place.

You are finding out which of the eight are load-bearing, and which are just 1989.
