Last week we described rooms. This week we ask why anybody needs one.

Baumeister and Leary's case is that belonging is a drive: it has the signature of
a fundamental motivation across cognition, emotion, health and behaviour, and
like a drive it can be satisfied. Their two conditions are what matter to us —
frequent, pleasant interaction, and a stable framework of ongoing mutual care.

Hold a third place against those two. It supplies the first magnificently: your
Tuesday regulars interact often and pleasantly and ask nothing of each other.
The second it supplies only partially, and sometimes not at all. Nobody at the
bar is going to drive you to a hospital appointment.

So the interesting question for the rest of the semester is what a setting that
delivers half of the requirement is actually worth. The satiation claim gives us
a way in: if belonging is satiable, then a small amount of the right kind of
contact should produce a large part of the benefit, and after some threshold
more contact should add little. That is a testable shape, and it is why the
regular who comes in twice a week may be getting most of what the regular who
comes in daily gets.

Bowlby is behind this week if you want the developmental account of where the
drive comes from. It is not required and it is not examined.
