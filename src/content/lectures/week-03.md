Cyberball should not work. Three players, a virtual ball, two of them stop
passing to you after a while. Participants who are told the other players are
computer programs still report the hit. Participants who are *paid* more the
more they are excluded still report the hit. Williams's account is that there is
a reflexive stage that arrives before you have had time to decide whether being
excluded here should matter — and it threatens four things at once: belonging,
self-esteem, control, and meaningful existence.

Then the point of the week. Go back and read Oldenburg's eight characteristics
and count the ones that describe who a place excludes. There are none. "Neutral
ground" and "a leveller" gesture at openness, but they describe the experience of
people already inside the room.

Every place you audited in week 1 has a boundary. Some of them are priced, some
are cultural, some are a door that is hard to find, and some are a set of
regulars who go quiet when a stranger walks in. The room does not have to intend
any of it. This is the lecture where the course stops being a celebration of
third places and starts being an investigation of them.
