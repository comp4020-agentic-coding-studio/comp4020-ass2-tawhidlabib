Steinkuehler and Williams do something this course has been building towards:
they take Oldenburg's eight characteristics and score a place against them, one
at a time, with fieldwork underneath each judgement. The place is a massively
multiplayer game, and it passes most of them. Neutral ground, a leveller,
conversation as the main activity, regulars, a playful mood — all present, some
of them more purely than in any pub.

Read the concessions carefully, because that is where the week lives.

Accessibility is not what Oldenburg meant. A room you enter by buying hardware,
paying a subscription and learning an interface has an entry cost, even if it is
open at four in the morning.

Neutral ground is not quite right either. A pub is owned by someone who can bar
you, but a game is owned by someone who can delete the room, change its physics
overnight, and read every word said in it.

And their capital finding — mostly bridging, not bonding — lands exactly on
week 6. Weak ties, manufactured at scale, by a company.

So the honest verdict is that the game satisfies the rules and the rules were not
built to catch what is different about it. That is a result about Oldenburg as
much as about the game, and it is the last time in this course that we use the
eight rules as a scoring sheet without arguing with them.
