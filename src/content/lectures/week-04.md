The minimal group studies are still astonishing. Divide people by a coin toss,
by a preference between two painters they have never heard of, and they will
allocate rewards to favour their own side — and will accept less for their own
group in absolute terms if it widens the gap.

If that is all it takes, then a bar counter is enormously more than enough. The
counter is the piece of furniture this lecture is about: a shared surface, a
fixed number of places, everyone facing the same way, a member of staff standing
opposite who mediates every arrival. It is a machine for turning attendance into
membership.

Social identity theory gets us from *I go there often* to *it is part of who I
am*, and it also gets us something less comfortable. If part of your self-concept
is made of a group, you are motivated to keep that group favourably distinct. The
warmth a regular feels toward the other regulars and the coolness they show a
stranger are not two separate facts about the room. They are the same mechanism,
seen from inside and outside.

That is the week 3 problem again, with a theory attached to it. A place cannot
generate belonging without generating a boundary, and the friendlier the inside,
the sharper the edge.
