Welcome back. Five weeks in, you have been describing rooms; this week is about
the shape of what the rooms produce.

Granovetter's argument is structural and it is almost arithmetic. Your close
friends know each other and largely know what you know. The person you see once
a month is embedded in a different cluster, and is therefore your only route to
information that is not already circulating in yours. Hence the finding: people
got jobs through acquaintances, not through close friends.

Now put that next to the third place. The man at the end of the bar whose
surname you do not know is not a failed friendship. He is a bridge, and the room
is a bridge factory. It manufactures exactly the tie that Granovetter says does
the work — repeated, low-obligation, and reaching somewhere your own cluster does
not.

This reframes what the whole semester has been looking at. A third place is not
primarily a source of intimacy. Week 2 already told us it delivers frequent
pleasant contact and not much ongoing mutual care. Granovetter tells us that the
half it delivers is the structurally valuable one, and that we have been
mis-valuing these rooms by measuring them against friendship.

Thursday you map your own ties and find out where each one was made. Most people
discover the answer is a place they have stopped going to. That finding is
week 8, arriving early.
