**Choose a room you are already in.** That is the cleanest position: you are a
member, you are describing the place you are part of, and you are not gathering
data on a community that has never heard of you. If you want to use a space you
do not belong to, read its rules first, check whether it is public in the sense
its members think it is, and clear it with Noor before you start.

**No verbatim quotes. No usernames. Not even in your own notes.** "A member who
had been in the server about a month asked a question that nobody answered for
two hours" is a finding. The sentence they actually typed is theirs. A public
channel is not the same thing as a consenting participant, and the archive means
anything you copy stays copyable.

**Sample a fixed window.** Pick the times before you look — say 19:00 to 21:00
on three days — and describe what happened in those windows. Scrolling until you
find something interesting will find something interesting, and it will tell you
nothing about the room.

**Count what week 7 taught you to count.** Mingling, observing, lingering.
Lingering is the dominant behaviour in most of these rooms and it is invisible:
the members who are present, reading, and never posting. Note that you cannot see
them, and note what that does to your count.

**Then go back a year in the archive, to the same window.** No other Round in
this course can do this. You have a third place that retained a complete record
of itself, so use it: is the room busier, quieter, or the same, and are the
people the same people? Week 8 needed directories and aerial photographs to ask
that question about a suburb. Here it takes ten minutes, and the ease of it is
worth a paragraph in itself.
