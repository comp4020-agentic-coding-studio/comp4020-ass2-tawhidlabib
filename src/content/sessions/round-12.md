Ten minutes, five of questions. No requirement to use slides, and the strongest
presentations in previous years have been a floor plan and a person talking.

**Name a real site.** An address, a building, a piece of ground, a server. Not "a
community space". If you cannot point at where it is, you have not done the
exercise.

**Name the cost of entry,** in money and in everything else — the skill, the
fluency, the confidence to walk in the first time. Week 9 taught you that a door
is not the only way to keep people out.

**Name the thing people are actually there to do.** The lecture's closing
argument is that belonging arrives as a by-product. If your answer to "why would
anyone come" is "to belong", expect that to be the first question.

**Say who it excludes.** Every place has a boundary and you have known this since
week 3. A proposal that claims to keep nobody out is not a generous proposal, it
is an unexamined one. The good answer names the boundary and argues that it is
worth its cost.

**Expect to be asked for evidence,** and expect the questioner to have read the
same twelve papers you did. Week 11 was practice for this.

The room has watched pubs, barbershops, laundromats, platforms and guild halls
for a semester. Use them. The best proposals have always been the ones that
sounded slightly unambitious and were clearly going to work.
