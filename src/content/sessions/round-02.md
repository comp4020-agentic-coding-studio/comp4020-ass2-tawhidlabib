Same place. Same day of the week. Same hour. This is the first thing most
fieldwork gets wrong, and it is easy to get right: if you change the time, you
have not observed the place twice, you have observed two places once each.

Before you go in, write down your rule. What will make you count somebody as a
regular? Recognising them from last week? The staff greeting them by name? Them
not looking at the menu? Whatever it is, commit to it on paper first, because a
rule you invent while counting will bend towards the answer you expected.

Then count. Total heads. How many you recognise. How many are alone. How many
the staff speak to unprompted.

Afterwards, find the case your rule got wrong — there is always one. The person
who was clearly a fixture and failed your test, or the stranger who passed it.
That failure is the useful part of the exercise. Baumeister and Leary need
*frequent* interaction, and you have just discovered how hard frequency is to
see from inside a single room.

Bring both numbers and the broken case.
