A new place this week, not your week 1 one. It must have a counter and somebody
working behind it.

Sit where you can see the door and the counter at the same time. This matters
more than anything else in the brief: the ten seconds after someone walks in
contain most of what you came to see.

**Log every arrival.** Time. Alone or not. What the staff did first — nothing, a
nod, a name, a drink already being poured. Where they went. Whether anyone
already there acknowledged them.

**Then the seats.** At the busiest moment, photograph nothing and instead draw
the room and mark which seats were empty. Empty seats in a full room are the
clearest evidence a place has a structure, and people will defend that structure
without being able to describe it.

**Two moments to bring back.** One where the counter levelled somebody — rank,
money or age stopped mattering for a moment. One where a boundary showed —
a conversation that closed, a look, a regular's seat defended, a price that
decided who was in the room. Describe both as events, with what was said and
done. Not "the atmosphere was cliquey".

A note on conduct: you are in a business. Buy something, do not photograph
people, and if a member of staff asks what you are doing, tell them the truth.
