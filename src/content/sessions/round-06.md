No site visit. Bring a large sheet of paper.

**List twenty people.** Not your twenty closest — twenty you have spoken to in
the last year, deliberately including the ones you would not call friends. The
person at the desk near yours. Someone from a group chat. A neighbour.

**Annotate frequency honestly.** Weekly, monthly, a few times a year. Use your
messages to check rather than your impression, because your impression is
generous about people you like.

**Then the important column: where was this tie made?** Name the actual setting.
School. A job. A gym. A game. A pub. A course. A party at somebody's house.
"Online" is not an answer — which room, which server, which thread.

**Now count the settings you still go to.** This is the number the Round is
built around, and for most people it is small. You will find a cluster of ties
that all came from one place, and a good chance that place is one you left.

**One concrete case.** Find something that actually reached you — a job, a flat,
a diagnosis, a band, a person — through somebody in the outer ring. Write down
the chain. Granovetter's claim is a claim about structure, and a single traced
chain is how it stops being abstract.

Bring the sheet. We compare the shape of the maps, and the shapes differ by
more than the number of people on them.
