This Round is indoors and it is not observation. You run Cyberball on each
other, in both conditions, and you rate your own four needs immediately after
each round.

**Opting out is free and private.** Tell either of us before we start, or simply
do not sit down; there is no penalty and nobody will ask why. This is a
manipulation designed to make people feel excluded, and consenting to it because
everyone else is in the room is not consent.

The debrief happens immediately after the last round and everybody stays for it.

Then the argument, which is the part that gets marked. You have just been
excluded by your classmates for four minutes and it measurably did something.
Scale that: the study has been run thousands of times on undergraduates. Was
that a reasonable thing for the field to do? It is a small, brief, fully
debriefed harm that has produced most of what we know about what exclusion costs.
Write your position down before you hear anyone else's — you will need it again
in week 7, when you start observing strangers who have not consented to anything.
