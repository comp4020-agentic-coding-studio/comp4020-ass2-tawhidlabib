Bring a laptop. This Round has no site.

**Find the claim in the wild.** A news article, a government campaign, a charity
page, a minister's speech, a LinkedIn post. Something that cites the loneliness
figure at the public. Save the exact wording.

**Trace it back.** Follow it to the paper and find the actual number. You will
usually find one of three things: the figure is quoted correctly, the effect size
has been converted into something snappier along the way, or the chain of
citation does not reach a paper at all.

**Write the two comparisons side by side.** What Holt-Lunstad and colleagues
compared, and what the headline compared. The gap between those two sentences is
the exercise.

**Three confounds.** Name them, and then go back to the paper and find out which
ones the authors addressed and how. They are not naive about this; the discussion
does real work, and the honest criticism is narrower than the lazy one.

**Then write the defensible version,** in no more than four sentences, as if you
had to say it to someone who would push back. Not a hedge that says nothing, and
not the headline. This is the exact skill your Place Audit needed and the one
your week 12 proposal will need, because you are about to argue that somewhere
should exist and someone will ask you what the evidence is.
