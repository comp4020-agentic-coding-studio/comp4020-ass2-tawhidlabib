  What exclusion does, measured in a lab with a ball game so trivial it should

Cyberball is three players tossing a virtual ball. After a few throws, two of
them stop passing to you. That is the whole manipulation, and it reliably
damages mood and threatens four needs at once: belonging, self-esteem, control
and meaningful existence. It works when participants know the other players are
computers. It works when they are told they will be *paid* to be excluded.

Read it for the reflexive stage — the hurt that arrives before any appraisal of
whether it should. Week 3 runs Cyberball on each other, and then asks the
question that matters here: a third place is defined partly by who it does not
let in, and Oldenburg's eight rules are silent about that.
