description:
  Social identity theory — how a group becomes part of who you are, and why the
  minimal conditions for that are so shockingly minimal.
tags: ["identity", "groups"]

The minimal group studies found that people will favour their own group when the
group is defined by nothing at all — a coin toss, a preference between two
painters they have not heard of. From that, Tajfel and Turner build an account
in which part of your self-concept is made of the groups you belong to, and you
are motivated to keep those groups looking good by comparison.

Week 4 is about the counter — the bar, the café, the place where the staff know
your order. Being a regular is a social identity, one of the cheapest available,
and this reading is how you get from "I go there a lot" to "it is part of who I
am". It is also the reading that explains why a beloved local can be quietly
hostile to outsiders without anyone intending it.
