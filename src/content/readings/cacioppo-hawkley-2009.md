Loneliness is not the same as being alone: it is the perceived gap between the
relationships you have and the ones you want. Cacioppo and Hawkley show that
this perception puts the brain into a state of implicit hypervigilance for
social threat, which biases attention, distorts how you read other people, and
fragments sleep — and which then makes connection harder to achieve.

This runs alongside week 11 and is the answer to "but how would that kill
anyone". It also matters for your fieldwork: the lonely person in the room you
are watching is likely reading that room more suspiciously than you are.
