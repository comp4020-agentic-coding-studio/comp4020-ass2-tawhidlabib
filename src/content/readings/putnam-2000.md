League bowling collapsed while the number of people bowling rose. Putnam stacks
up decades of data on clubs, unions, churches, dinner parties and card games and
finds the same shape in all of them. His account of the causes — television,
suburban sprawl, two-career households, generational replacement — is still
argued about, and the generational finding is the one that has held up best.

Week 8 asks you to count what your own suburb lost, using whatever record
survives: old directories, council minutes, a newspaper archive, an aerial
photograph. Read Putnam for the method rather than the headline. The interesting
question is not whether things got worse but how you would know, and this book
is an extended answer to that.
